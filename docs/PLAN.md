# The Wild Robot Challenge: Project Plan

A five-day robotics, empathy and AI ethics challenge for Grade 8 students, built around *The Wild Robot* and the mBot2. Teams find a real problem in their community, and design and build a solution for it.

- **Audience:** Grade 8 (ages 13–14), working in teams
- **Format:** 5 days × 2 hours (10 hours in total)
- **Facilitators:** 2
- **Hardware:** mBot2 (CyberPi), programmed with mBlock
- **Long term:** a standalone "adventure" that can later be added to the new BitSized website

## Ground rules for the build

1. **Keep the original design.** The colours and layout stay as they are (`src/index.css`: orange `#f3961c`, azure `#1ca6f3`, dark text `#252525`, subtle background `#faf4f5`). Design improvements are suggested first and only built once they're approved.
2. **Small steps.** Each step ends with something that can be checked, and the next step starts only after approval.
3. **Clips:** official clips (e.g. DreamWorks' YouTube channel) plus timestamps for a licensed copy played in class. No self-hosted film footage.
4. **Children's privacy:** no student emails. Team accounts are created by the facilitators, and team names are used instead of real names.

## 1. Structure of each day

Each day covers two chapters of the film and follows the same seven steps:

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 🎬 Film moment | 10 min | Two official clips or timestamps |
| 🌍 Real world now | 10 min | A current news case showing the same idea |
| ❤️ Empathy talk | 10 min | Discussion of the day's empathy principle |
| 🤖 Build and code | 40 min | The CS concept, taught through mBot2 activities |
| ⚖️ Roz's Dilemma | 10 min | An AI ethics question linking the clip and the news case |
| 💡 Design move | 15 min | One design-thinking step applied to the team's own problem |
| ✂️ Make and check in | 25 min | Maker task, plus one submission for the facilitator dashboard |

**Threads that run through all five days:**
- **Solution Journal:** each day, every team adds a page about its own community problem. By Day 5 it records the team's whole design process.
- **Robot Code of Ethics:** each day, teams add rules from that day's dilemmas. Their final robot must follow their own code.
- **Design thinking:** Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test, spread across the week instead of taught as one separate lesson.

## 2. The five days

### Day 1: Awakening & Learning the Language
**Design stage:** Empathize (notice your first reactions, then observe before you act)
- 🎬 Roz activates and the animals see her as a monster. Roz observes the animals until she learns their language.
- ❤️ First impressions, and listening before acting.
- 🌍 **Humanoid robots coming into homes:** 1X NEO, partly operated remotely by humans at first.
  **Bonus:** AI decoding animal language (Project CETI's sperm whales, the Earth Species Project).
- 🤖 What is a robot? Sense–Plan–Act. Unbox the mBot2, show a team message on the CyberPi, and read distance and colour sensor values.
- ⚖️ *Should a robot have to tell you when a human is controlling it? Is having data the same as understanding?* (transparency, data vs. understanding)
- 💡 **Problem Hunter:** observe and list problems at school, in the community and in Canada.
- ✂️ Team name and badge; a first sketch of the community map.
- **Check-in:** the Problem Hunter list.
- *Reuses the existing Lesson 1 and Lesson 2 content.*

### Day 2: Task Acquired & Learning to Fly
**Design stage:** Empathize → Define (understand the people affected), with a prototype mindset
- 🎬 Brightbill hatches and Roz becomes his mother. Brightbill fails over and over before he flies.
- ❤️ Responsibility for someone else's needs; patience, and treating failure as part of learning.
- 🌍 **Care robots reducing loneliness** in older adults (a 2026 review; Singapore's Lovot trial).
  **Wildfire drones still being tested:** FireSwarm in B.C., and Alberta's infrared drones.
- 🤖 Algorithms and sequences (program a "food delivery" route); loops and debugging (fix a broken program).
- ⚖️ *Can a robot really care? When must a human stay in control?* (care and dependency, humans in the loop)
- 💡 Choose one problem. Build an empathy map or interview someone affected.
- ✂️ The base of the community model.
- **Check-in:** the chosen problem and empathy map.

### Day 3: Not What You Seem & The Migration
**Design stage:** Define → Ideate
- 🎬 Fink and Roz are judged as "the fox" and "the monster". Brightbill leads the migration.
- ❤️ Seeing past labels; supporting others to become independent.
- 🌍 **Facial recognition wrongful arrests** (2026 lawsuits; bias in training data).
  **Banff wildlife crossings:** fencing guides animals to crossings, the same way a line guides the mBot.
  **Waymo's school bus recall:** a rule the robot failed to follow.
- 🤖 Conditionals (if/else) for obstacle avoidance; line following with the Quad RGB sensor.
- ⚖️ *What happens when a robot's data is unfair? Who's responsible when a self-driving robot breaks a rule?* (bias, accountability)
- 💡 Write a "How might we…?" question, check it for assumptions, then come up with as many ideas as possible and choose one.
- ✂️ Add people, places and a path to the model.
- **Check-in:** the "How might we" question and the chosen idea.

### Day 4: The Winter Storm & More Than Programmed
**Design stage:** Prototype
- 🎬 Roz shelters the animals and the truce begins. Roz resists being reset and chooses who she wants to be.
- ❤️ Cooperation: a community solves problems together. Empathy is a choice.
- 🌍 **Calgary's Bearspaw water main break (January 2026):** the community saved water together, robots inspected the pipe and hit their limits, and warning signs had been ignored for years.
  **Canada's Bill C-34:** proposed safety rules for AI chatbots that children use.
- 🤖 Systems design: plan what the robot senses, decides and does, then code the prototype.
- ⚖️ *What can't technology fix without people? Who should control a robot: its maker, its owner, or itself?* (limits of technology, autonomy, ownership)
- 💡 Build the prototype: put the solution on the model and run it.
- ✂️ The solution build on the model.
- **Check-in:** a photo or video of the prototype running.

### Day 5: I Am Already Home
**Design stage:** Test → Share

This day uses a different schedule:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 min | 🎬 The ending: Roz belongs to the island · 🌍 a Calgary Youth Science Fair student's pipe-inspection robot, and robotic sage-grouse decoys in Grand Teton |
| 40 min | Test, gather feedback, improve |
| 15 min | Finish the Robot Code of Ethics |
| 40 min | Showcase: each team presents problem → empathy → solution → ethics |
| 15 min | Reflection and celebration |

**Check-in:** final presentation and reflection.

## 6. Deep mBot2 connection (future)

- **How it works:** the site talks to the robot from Chrome or Edge over USB (Web Serial) or Bluetooth (Web Bluetooth). This works on Windows, Mac and Chromebook, **but not on iPad or in Safari.**
- **What it needs:** a small "listener" program loaded once onto each CyberPi with mBlock, which takes commands from the site and sends sensor data back.
- **What it would add:** a "Connect robot" button, live sensor readings, remote-control buttons, and possibly a block editor later.
- **Steps:**
  1. A test version with one robot and one laptop
  2. The listener program
  3. The website screens
  4. Testing on school devices (IT may block USB or Bluetooth access)
  5. The light version kept as a backup if a robot won't connect
- **Effort:** several weeks. Only start after the challenge has run once with the light version.
